Kamapathy for Android

Kamapathy for Android Privacy Policy

Last updated 7 November 2026

Kamapathy for Android (app.kamapathy.browser) is a free web browser published by SocialSure Private Limited. You can pair it with Kamapathy on your computer, the desktop browser from kamapathy.app, to send tabs between the two. This policy explains what the Android app does with your information.

What we collect

Nothing. Kamapathy has no account, no ads, no analytics and no crash reporting, and we run no server for it. We, SocialSure Private Limited, receive no data about you, your browsing or your devices. In the terms of the Data safety section on Google Play, the app collects no data and shares no data. The rest of this policy says what Kamapathy keeps on your phone, what leaves it, and where that goes.

What stays on your phone

Kamapathy keeps the following in its own storage on your phone, which other apps can't read:

  • your spaces and their open tabs (each tab's address and title), and which tab you had open;
  • your bookmarks, and your history of the last 1000 pages you visited;
  • the list of your recent downloads;
  • your answers to sites that asked to use your camera, microphone, location or MIDI devices;
  • your settings;
  • what websites store, such as cookies, site data and their cache, kept apart for each space with its own sign-ins and shared by the spaces that share sign-ins;
  • once you pair with your computer: its name, its addresses on your network, the keys that let the two recognise each other, the tabs waiting to be sent to it, a record of which tabs it sent you so that none opens twice, and the addresses of the last 50 pages it offered you to continue, so that none is offered twice.

Your computer's list of open tabs is kept in memory only while Kamapathy is open, and is never saved. Pages you open from your computer's local pages are never added to your history. Files you download go to your phone's Downloads folder, where they stay like any other download. Android backup and transfer to a new phone are turned off for Kamapathy, so none of this goes to a cloud backup.

What leaves your phone

The pages you visit. Like any browser, Kamapathy sends your requests to the sites you open. They receive what browsers send them, such as your IP address and the cookies they set, and each handles that under its own privacy policy. Kamapathy blocks third-party cookies.

Searches. What you type in the address bar stays on your phone until you submit it. Suggestions come from your own history, bookmarks and tabs, and from your computer's tabs once you have paired. A search you submit goes to your search engine: DuckDuckGo, unless you choose Google or Bing in Settings, Browsing.

Safe Browsing. Safe Browsing is on unless you turn it off in Settings, Browsing. While it is on, Android System WebView, the part of Android that shows web pages in Kamapathy, checks the pages you open against Google Safe Browsing's lists of unsafe sites, with Google Play services on your phone, and shows a warning instead of a page that is on those lists. Google makes these checks under its own privacy policy, at https://policies.google.com/privacy. We receive nothing from them. Kamapathy also tells Android System WebView not to send Google its diagnostic data for this app.

Your paired computer. Pairing is optional. When you pair your phone with Kamapathy on your computer, the two talk directly over your local network, such as your Wi-Fi or your phone's hotspot. The connection is encrypted with TLS 1.3, each device accepts only the other's key, and nothing passes through a server or through us. The code your computer shows is a link to kamapathy.app/pair whose pairing details come after its # sign, the part of a link that is never sent to a server. Your phone sends your computer:

  • its name (the device name in Android's settings, or else its model), its model, and its Android and Kamapathy versions;
  • the tabs you send to it;
  • while “Share this phone's open tabs” is on in Settings, Laptop: the address and title of each open tab, and the names and colours of your spaces.

Your computer keeps your phone's name and model, and when it last connected, until the pairing ends, and keeps your phone's list of tabs in memory only. It sends your phone its name, its addresses on your network, the tabs you send from it and, while it shares them, its open tabs and which one is in front. A tab your computer sends waits there for up to 7 days, until you next open Kamapathy on your phone. A tab you send from your phone waits on your phone for up to 7 days, until your computer is reachable.

Your computer's local pages. When you open a page your computer serves on localhost, such as a site you are building, Kamapathy loads it through your paired computer over the same encrypted connection. Your computer opens only the pages you send from it and the ones you allow there when your phone asks. While such a page is open, Kamapathy runs a small proxy on your phone that only its tabs for your computer's local pages can use, and it carries their traffic only to your computer. These tabs keep their cookies and site data apart from your other tabs, and Kamapathy clears that data when you forget your computer.

What you choose to share. When you share a page, open a link in another app or upload a file, it goes to the app or site you chose, under that app's or site's own terms.

Passwords. Kamapathy keeps no passwords. Android's autofill lets the password manager you chose fill sign-in forms in Kamapathy, under that password manager's own terms.

Permissions

Kamapathy asks for the camera, the microphone and your location only when you first need them, never at launch. For a site, Android asks only after you have allowed that site in Kamapathy's own prompt, and you can remove your answers in Settings, Site permissions.

  • INTERNET: to load web pages and to reach your paired computer.
  • ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE: to know which Wi-Fi your phone is on, so that it can reach your computer over it.
  • CAMERA: to scan the code your computer shows when you pair, and for sites you allow to use your camera. Kamapathy reads the code on your phone and keeps no camera frames.
  • RECORD_AUDIO: for sites you allow to use your microphone.
  • MODIFY_AUDIO_SETTINGS: so that sites you allow to use your microphone can set up audio, such as for a call.
  • ACCESS_COARSE_LOCATION and ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION: for sites you allow to know your location, and only while you use Kamapathy. Kamapathy doesn't use your location itself.

It also declares app.kamapathy.browser.DYNAMIC_RECEIVER_NOT_EXPORTED_PERMISSION, which Android's support library (AndroidX) adds so that other apps can't send messages to Kamapathy's internal receivers. It gives no access to your data or to any device feature.

Kamapathy doesn't ask for notifications, storage, contacts, background location, nearby devices or the advertising ID.

Your choices and deleting your data

  • Clear history, in History, deletes your history.
  • Clear this space's data, or Clear data for all spaces with shared sign-ins, in a space's settings, deletes cookies, site data and the cache.
  • Remove deletes a bookmark or a site permission, and Remove all, in Settings, Site permissions, deletes every site permission.
  • Deleting a space deletes its tabs, and the cookies and site data of a space with its own sign-ins.
  • In Settings, Laptop, turning off “Share this phone's open tabs” stops your phone sending its tabs to your computer, and turning off “Offer to continue from your laptop” stops those offers.
  • Forget, in Settings, Laptop, ends the pairing: Kamapathy deletes its pairing key, your computer's details, the tabs waiting to be sent and the data of your computer's local pages and, if your computer is connected at that moment, tells it to forget this phone too. You can also forget the phone in Kamapathy on your computer, in Settings, Phone.
  • Uninstalling Kamapathy deletes everything it keeps on your phone. Files you downloaded stay in Downloads until you delete them.

We keep no copy of any of this, so there is nothing for us to delete or hand over on request.

Security

Pairing is checked on both screens: each shows the same six-digit number, and neither device keeps the pairing until you confirm on it. Everything between your phone and your computer is encrypted with TLS 1.3, and each device accepts only the key it paired with. The key your phone uses for pairing is made and kept in Android's secure key storage, and it can't be copied out. Kamapathy never opens a site whose certificate isn't valid.

Children

Kamapathy is meant for adults and is not directed at children. It collects no data from anyone, children included.
