How it plays

Three ways to make noise. One way to stay alive.

01 · tap

click

Three meters of wall, faintly, for two seconds. Cheap. Quiet. Barely enough.

02 · hold

chirp

Eight meters, clearly. The map you actually need, and the sound that wakes a bristlemaw two tunnels over.

03 · hold longer

shout

The whole chamber, for six seconds. It echoes. Everything hears it. The Warden gets faster, permanently.

Then the walls fade, bright to dim to nothing, and you swim through what you remember, one thumb on the glass, a soft buzz when you graze a surface. Predators hunt by hearing. Drift silently, throw a pebble somewhere else, and evolve new senses between runs.

Eyes closed mode

This is not an accessibility toggle bolted on at the end. Sound and touch are the canonical game. Sighted players are the ones adapting.

  • spatial audioPredator clicks, lure hums, the downdraft of the exit: every sound is panned and attenuated by where it actually is. Drips are pitched to the size of the room.
  • haptic languageWall brush, predator bearing, current, damage: a designed vocabulary with amplitude control on devices that support it, and a calibration slider on the rest.
  • spoken cuesIn Eyes Closed mode, predator bearings are spoken through the screen reader: “clicks, ahead-left, close.” Menus are real buttons, navigable with TalkBack.
  • no flashingNothing on screen ever flashes above threshold. The brightest thing in the game is a line.

The grotto

Evolve senses, not stats.

Death is permanent for the run. You keep otoliths, ear stones, and spend them between dives. Every evolution changes how the cave feels, and you can long-press to feel one before you buy it.
